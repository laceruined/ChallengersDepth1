CHALLENGER'S DEPTH / EXPEDITION 01

DIVE
DOWN.

Welcome aboard. Scroll downward to take a journey through the different layers of the ocean. Meet creatures from the bright surface all the way to the darkness of the deep sea.

SCROLL TO BEGIN THE DIVE ↓
00 — 200 METERS

The
Sunlit
Ocean

The journey begins in the part of the ocean that receives sunlight. This is where enormous whales travel, sea turtles migrate, coral reefs grow, and countless fish search for food. Look around carefully. The ocean is already full of life.

SPECIES 001
Orca
Orcas, also known as killer whales, are toothed whales and the largest members of the oceanic dolphin family. They are apex predators with diverse diets and highly sophisticated hunting techniques.
SPECIES 002
Sea Lion
Sea lions are aquatic mammals with external ear flaps, long foreflippers, short hair, and powerful bodies. They live throughout much of the world's oceans.
SPECIES 003
Green Sea Turtle
Green sea turtles are large migratory marine reptiles found in tropical and subtropical waters worldwide. Adults feed mainly on seagrass and algae.
200M — LIGHT BEGINS TO FADE ↓
200 — 1,000 METERS

The
Twilight
Zone

Sunlight becomes weaker here. The blue of the surface slowly disappears. Many creatures living in this region have developed incredible adaptations. Some migrate hundreds of meters every day. Others use camouflage. Some create their own light.

SPECIES 004
Barreleye Fish
Barreleye fish have tubular, barrel-shaped eyes that can point upward to detect silhouettes of prey. Their transparent head makes them one of the strangest fish in the ocean.
SPECIES 005
Octopus
Octopuses are masters of camouflage and are known for remarkable intelligence, problem-solving abilities, and complex behavior.
SPECIES 006
Seahorse
Seahorses are unusual fish with upright bodies, curled tails, and independently moving eyes. In many species, males carry developing young.
1,000M — SUNLIGHT DISAPPEARS COMPLETELY
1,000 — 4,000 METERS

The
Midnight
Zone

There is no sunlight here. Plants cannot use photosynthesis. The ocean becomes almost completely dark. Yet this is one of the most fascinating environments on Earth. Many animals produce their own light. This phenomenon is called bioluminescence.

SPECIES 007
Faceless Fish
The faceless cusk is a deep-sea cusk-eel that can live thousands of meters below the surface. Its strange appearance comes from the unusual position of its mouth and eyes.
SPECIES 008
Angler Fish
Anglerfish are famous deep-sea predators that use a glowing lure to attract prey. Their bioluminescent lure is one of the most recognizable adaptations in the deep ocean.
SPECIES 009
Abyssal Spiderfish
The abyssal spiderfish uses long, specialized fin rays to stand above the ocean floor like a tripod.
4,000M — ENTERING THE ABYSS ↓
4,000 — 6,000 METERS

The
Abyss

Welcome to one of Earth's harshest environments. Temperatures are close to freezing. Pressure is enormous. Food is scarce. And yet life survives. Animals here have evolved to survive conditions that would be impossible for humans without a specialized vehicle.

SPECIES 010
Grenadiers
Grenadiers, also known as rattails, are deep-sea fish found from the Arctic to Antarctic. They are among the most abundant deep-sea fish families.
SPECIES 011
Comb Jelly
Comb jellies are marine invertebrates that move using rows of tiny cilia. Their bodies can produce beautiful rainbow-like displays as light scatters through the cilia.
6,000M — THE HADAL ZONE
6,000 METERS AND BELOW

The
Hadal
Zone

You have reached the deepest region of the ocean. The name comes from Hades, the ancient Greek god of the underworld. These depths are found inside deep ocean trenches. One of the most famous is the Mariana Trench. At its deepest point lies Challenger Deep.

SPECIES 012
Hadal Snailfish
Hadal snailfish are gelatinous deep-sea fish adapted to the crushing pressure of ocean trenches. Some species live at depths exceeding 8,000 meters.
SPECIES 013
Cusk Eel
Cusk eels are marine bony fish that include species adapted to extremely deep environments. Some members of the group can grow to impressive lengths.
END OF EXPEDITION

You reached
the deep.

But this is only the beginning. Scientists have explored only a small fraction of the world's oceans. There are still creatures, ecosystems, shipwrecks, trenches, and geological mysteries waiting to be discovered.
