03 / The Deep

CHALLENGER
DEPTH

Nearly eleven kilometers beneath the Pacific Ocean lies a place so remote that only a small number of humans have ever seen it.

This is the story of the deepest known point on Earth — and the people who dared to descend.

Where Earth disappears

10,935 meters
below the surface.

Challenger Deep lies within the Mariana Trench in the western Pacific, near Guam.

NOAA's current cited estimate places the deepest observed seafloor at approximately 10,935 meters below mean sea level.

At that depth there is no sunlight, temperatures hover just above freezing, and the pressure is more than a thousand times the pressure at sea level.

01 / A history of descent

Seven miles down.

Challenger Deep has been visited by research vehicles, robots and a remarkably small group of human explorers. Here are some of the milestones that changed what we know about the deepest ocean.

1875

HMS Challenger

The expedition of HMS Challenger made some of the first soundings of the Mariana Trench. The deepest region would later become known as Challenger Deep.

FIRST SOUNDINGS
1960

Trieste

Jacques Piccard and U.S. Navy Lieutenant Don Walsh descended inside the bathyscaphe Trieste. They became the first humans to reach Challenger Deep.

FIRST HUMANS
1995

Kaiko

Japan's remotely operated vehicle Kaiko reached Challenger Deep, bringing robotic exploration back to the deepest part of the ocean.

ROBOTIC EXPLORATION
2009

Nereus

Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution's hybrid underwater vehicle Nereus reached Challenger Deep and collected observations from the seafloor.

HYBRID VEHICLE
2012

James Cameron

Cameron became the first person to make a solo descent to Challenger Deep in the specially designed Deepsea Challenger.

SOLO DESCENT
2019

Five Deeps

Victor Vescovo and the Five Deeps team conducted repeated dives using the DSV Limiting Factor.

REPEATED DIVES
2020

Ring of Fire

Victor Vescovo and Dr. Kathy Sullivan descended together, while NOAA-supported research gathered pressure and acoustic data from the deepest ocean.

SCIENCE + EXPLORATION
2021

More explorers

Richard Garriott, Michael Dubno, Hamish Harding and Nicole Yamase were among the passengers taken to Challenger Deep during a new series of dives.

NEW GENERATION
02 / The people

The humans
who went down.

Reaching Challenger Deep requires more than courage. It requires pressure-resistant engineering, navigation, life-support systems, and an enormous team working on the surface.

1960

Jacques Piccard

Swiss ocean engineer and explorer. Co-pilot of Trieste during the first human descent.

1960

Don Walsh

U.S. Navy lieutenant and oceanographer. He piloted Trieste alongside Piccard.

2012

James Cameron

Filmmaker and explorer who completed the first solo descent to Challenger Deep.

2019+

Victor Vescovo

Explorer and pilot of Limiting Factor, who made repeated descents and led the Five Deeps expedition.

2020

Kathy Sullivan

Former NASA astronaut and geologist who became the first woman to reach Challenger Deep.

2021

Richard Garriott

Explorer and game-industry pioneer who visited Challenger Deep as a mission specialist.

2021

Hamish Harding

Explorer and aviation entrepreneur who descended with Vescovo in March 2021.

2021

Nicole Yamase

Marine scientist from the Federated States of Micronesia who descended to the western pool.

26 / 03 / 2012

Alone at the bottom.

James Cameron spent years developing the Deepsea Challenger, a specially designed deep-submergence vehicle built for extreme depth.

On March 26, 2012, he descended alone into Challenger Deep, becoming the first person to make a solo descent.

The expedition brought cameras, sampling equipment and scientific instruments into a part of the ocean that humans had barely seen before.
