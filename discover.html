01 / MARINE LIFE

The ocean is stranger than fiction.

From enormous whales to almost invisible jellyfish, the ocean is home to life that looks like it belongs on another planet. Meet some of its most fascinating inhabitants.

SPECIES 001

The Blue Whale

The blue whale is the largest animal known to have ever lived. Its enormous body can stretch longer than a basketball court.

SPECIES 002

The Three-Hearted Octopus

Octopuses have three hearts and blue blood. Two hearts move blood through the gills, while the third sends it around the body.

SPECIES 003

Ancient Jellyfish

Jellyfish have drifted through Earth's oceans for hundreds of millions of years, surviving enormous changes to the planet.

SPECIES 004

Ancient Sharks

Sharks have existed for hundreds of millions of years, long before humans appeared on Earth.

SPECIES 005

Ocean Navigators

Sea turtles can travel thousands of kilometres across open water and return to important coastal areas.

SPECIES 006

Life Without Sunlight

Some deep-sea ecosystems survive without sunlight, relying on chemical energy from hydrothermal vents.

THE BIGGER PICTURE

We have only seen a fraction of the ocean.

Vast parts of the underwater world remain difficult to explore. Every expedition has the potential to reveal species, ecosystems and landscapes that have never been seen before.

02 / LIFE BELOW

The ocean
never sleeps.

Beneath the surface, life follows its own rhythm. Some creatures hunt in daylight, others rise from the darkness, and deep below, an entirely different world continues in almost complete darkness.

THE SURFACE

A world of light.

Sunlight powers enormous ecosystems. Coral reefs, fish, turtles and whales all occupy the brighter upper ocean.

AFTER DARK

The migration begins.

When darkness arrives, countless animals travel upward from deeper water to feed before disappearing again.

THE DEEP

A world without sun.

Far below the surface, darkness is permanent. Bioluminescent creatures create their own flashes of light.
